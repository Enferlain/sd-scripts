Understanding the Distributions

📊 Noise Schedule

The left chart shows the forward diffusion process: signal √α̅ₜ decays while noise √(1-α̅ₜ) grows. At t=0 (clean data) signal is 1.0; at t=T (pure noise) it approaches 0. This defines the continuous corruption path your model must learn to reverse.

📈 Sampling Distribution

The right chart displays which timesteps are actually being sampled during training. Uniform sampling draws all t equally; logit-normal (SD3-style) concentrates on mid-range timesteps; loss-aware rebalancing favors harder steps. Mismatches indicate sampler bugs or DDP imbalance.

💡 Why It Matters

Non-uniform sampling steers training compute toward timesteps where the model struggles. High-noise steps (large t) require predicting huge vectors; mid-range steps balance signal/noise gradients. Monitoring this curve helps validate schedule changes, detect drift, and diagnose convergence issues.
